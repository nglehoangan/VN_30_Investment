## 5. Preserve economic objective

BQ-MOAT evaluates whether the company possesses competitive advantages that create and protect meaningful economic benefits.

Assess three economic dimensions:

1. Advantage.
2. Economic Behavior.
3. Persistence.

These dimensions support one holistic assessment.

Do not divide the six points into three independent two-point components.

Do not introduce a mechanical weighted-indicator formula.

The analyst selects an existing assessment band based on economically sufficient evidence.

The executable validates that assessment.

## 6. Approved evidence sufficiency contract

### 6.1 Advantage — CORE

Require evidence establishing the existence, absence or limitations of competitive advantages.

Recognized advantage types include:

- Brand.
- Distribution.
- Switching costs.
- Cost advantage.
- Network effects.
- Licenses and regulatory barriers.
- Scale.
- Proprietary data and know-how.
- Customer stickiness and ecosystem control.

Evaluate only economically relevant advantage types.

Do not require every type to exist.

Do not require a separate evidence product for inapplicable advantage types.

For low bands, affirmative evidence of weak differentiation, competitive disadvantage or narrow advantages may satisfy this dimension.

Missing information alone does not establish competitive disadvantage.

### 6.2 Economic Behavior — CORE

Require evidence linking the identified advantage or disadvantage to observable economic or customer behavior.

Permitted evidence includes:

- Sustainable operating profitability.
- Relevant margin trends.
- Repeat business.
- Contract renewals.
- Customer relationships.
- Contract duration.
- Project execution.
- Pricing behavior.
- Relative operating efficiency.
- Productivity.
- Distribution effectiveness.
- Market participation and customer demand.
- Other sector-equivalent operating observations.

Require a documented economic connection between the identified advantage and observed outcomes.

Do not automatically infer moat from:

- High ROIC.
- High ROE.
- High EBIT margin.
- Revenue growth.
- Large market share.
- Company size.
- Employee count.
- Management claims.

For low bands, evidence of commodity economics, weak differentiation or competitive disadvantages may support the assessment.

### 6.3 Persistence — BAND-DEPENDENT

Persistence must be evaluated proportionately to the selected band.

For scores 0–2:

- Require sufficient evidence of competitive disadvantage, minimal advantage or narrow advantage.
- Do not require proof of durable superiority.
- Do not assign a low band merely because long-term persistence information is unavailable.

For score 3:

- Require supported evidence of actual differentiation.
- Allow durability to remain uncertain.
- Explicitly describe the persistence limitation.
- Do not require the same durability evidence used for scores 5–6.

For score 4:

- Require economically meaningful competitive advantage.
- Require evidence that the advantage has persisted sufficiently to support this band.
- Recognize that the advantage remains contestable.

For score 5:

- Require strong competitive advantage and demonstrated multi-year durability.
- Require credible economic and behavioral evidence.
- Address material competitive counterevidence.

For score 6:

- Require multiple durable competitive advantages.
- Preserve existing maximum prerequisites.
- Require exceptional justification.
- Require valid disconfirming evidence review.
- Demonstrate persistence through economic and behavioral evidence.

Do not introduce arbitrary mandatory numerical thresholds or fixed observation counts beyond existing approved requirements.

For lower bands, uncertainty concerning persistence must not automatically create N/R if the selected band remains economically defensible.

## 7. Public evidence and approved proxies

Use retained public evidence first.

Preferred sources:

- Audited financial statements.
- Annual reports.
- Segment reporting.
- AGM materials.
- Official issuer disclosures.
- Public industry information.
- Relevant competitor disclosures.
- Other sources already authorized by existing source policy.

The following are permitted alternative evidence routes:

| Difficult-to-access information | Permitted alternative evidence |
|---|---|
| Exact customer churn | Repeat business, renewal history, long-standing relationships |
| Quantified switching costs | System integration, contract duration, implementation complexity |
| Customer lifetime value | Observable economic outcomes from sustained customer relationships |
| Internal customer acquisition cost | Sales efficiency, selling expense and relevant growth evidence |
| Competitor internal cost structure | Comparable margins, productivity and operating efficiency |
| Proprietary technology details | Demonstrated capabilities, products, certifications and project execution |
| Exact market share | Properly scoped relative position or comparable market participation |
| Brand valuation | Observable demand, distribution and pricing evidence |

These alternatives are evidence options, not mandatory checklists.

Do not require every alternative to be available.

Do not misrepresent proxy observations as directly measured internal metrics.

For example:

- Revenue growth does not establish an exact retention rate.
- Stable margins do not prove switching costs.
- Company size does not independently establish a moat.
- Certifications do not automatically establish economic superiority.

Document the economic mechanism, limitations and material counterevidence.

Once sufficient approved evidence supports a defensible band, stop searching for inaccessible original information.
