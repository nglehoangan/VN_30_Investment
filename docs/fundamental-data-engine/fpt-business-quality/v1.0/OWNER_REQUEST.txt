## 5. BQ-CASH methodology

### 5.1 Preserve economic objective

Assess recurring economic realization and earnings recognition quality.

Do not divide the six points into additive sub-metric points.

Use one holistic rubric assessment.

### 5.2 Evidence classification

- Realization: CORE.
- Recognition: CORE.
- One-offs: CONDITIONAL according to economic materiality.
- Working capital: CONDITIONAL according to economic materiality.
- Maintenance versus growth CAPEX: SUPPORTING or CONDITIONAL.
- Exceptional recurring realization and adjustment quality: MAXIMUM_ONLY.

A supporting or conditional topic must not automatically block an otherwise defensible band when it is economically immaterial.

Require explicit materiality rationale.

Do not ignore material working-capital deterioration, abnormal gains or recognition problems.

### 5.3 Financial calculations

Use FY2022–FY2025 retained financial reports.

Calculate where applicable:

- Revenue.
- Reported NPAT.
- Normalized NPAT.
- CFO.
- Total CAPEX.
- FCF.
- Cumulative CFO/normalized NPAT.
- Cumulative FCF/normalized NPAT.
- Accounts receivable.
- Inventory.
- Accounts payable.
- Relevant working-capital movements.
- Material accruals and noncash gains.
- Material one-off adjustments.

Use:

Cumulative CFO conversion = sum of qualified CFO / sum of comparable normalized NPAT.

FCF = CFO minus appropriately defined CAPEX cash outflows.

Maintain consistent cash-flow and earnings scope.

Do not combine consolidated CFO with an incompatible parent-only earnings denominator.

Use meaningful sector-equivalent denominators when authorized.

Do not calculate misleading ratios using negative, near-zero or economically incompatible earnings denominators.

Use the underlying economic observations and approved alternatives instead.

### 5.4 Existing economic anchors

Preserve cumulative 3–5 year CFO/NPAT signals:

- At least 110%: strong.
- 90–110%: good.
- 70–90%: watch.
- 50–70%: weak.
- Below 50%: poor.

Treat these as economic signals, not automatic point assignments.

Interpret cash realization alongside working capital, recognition quality, business characteristics and material adjustments.

### 5.5 CAPEX limitations

Distinguish maintenance and growth CAPEX where supported.

If the split is unavailable:

- Use total CAPEX.
- Report total-capex FCF.
- Record the limitation.
- Use available investment disclosures where economically relevant.
- Do not invent maintenance-capex estimates.
- Do not automatically assign N/R solely because the split is unavailable.

If the distinction is essential to the selected band, apply the approved conditional materiality rule.

### 5.6 Normalization and recognition

Review material:

- Noncash gains.
- Fair-value movements.
- Provision reversals.
- Asset-disposal income.
- Capitalized costs.
- Related-party receivables.
- Repeated abnormal earnings.
- Other material recognition issues.

Do not assume the absence of material abnormal items without reviewing relevant evidence.

Do not require every possible abnormal item to have a separately populated metric.

### 5.7 Sector equivalents

Preserve existing approved sector-specific methods.

For banks, use economic recognition and realization evidence including asset quality, provisioning, accrued interest, credit cost and recurring income.

Do not apply industrial CFO/NPAT mechanically to banks.

For property developers, use relevant collections, handover conversion, inventory monetization and receivables quality.

The actual pilot remains FPT.

### 5.8 Preserve existing bands

Use the exact canonical labels and points:

- 0: Persistent failure of economic realization.
- 1–2: Weak realization with recurring adverse adjustments.
- 3–4: Adequate realization with material qualifications.
- 5–6: Strong recurring realization and adjustment quality.

Preserve within-band stronger-evidence requirements.

Select conservative points only within an economically defensible band.

Do not use a low band as a missing-data substitute.

Maximum 6/6 requires recurring realization, transparent adjustments and sound recognition.

Preserve existing exceptional justification and counterevidence controls.

A high cash-conversion ratio alone is insufficient.

## 6. BQ-RES methodology

### 6.1 Preserve economic objective

Assess the business model's resilience against adverse economic and operating developments.

Do not confuse business resilience with current balance-sheet strength or competitive advantage.

### 6.2 Evidence classification

- Concentration: CORE.
- Cyclicality: CORE.
- Pricing power: SUPPORTING or CONDITIONAL according to materiality.
- Stress behavior: BAND-DEPENDENT.
- Proven franchise protection without distressed capital actions during severe plausible stress: MAXIMUM_ONLY.

Require sufficient evidence about material vulnerabilities and resilience mechanisms.

Do not require all possible concentration dimensions to have numeric data.

### 6.3 Concentration review

Evaluate economically relevant concentration across:

- Customers.
- Products.
- Suppliers.
- Geographic markets.
- Projects.
- Business segments.
- Funding dependencies where relevant to business-model resilience.

Use existing disclosed customer concentration anchors:

- No single customer above 10%: generally positive.
- Above 20%: material risk requiring contextual review.
- Above 30%: major concentration requiring explicit mitigation.

These are economic signals, not automatic points.

When exact customer shares are unavailable, use qualified alternative evidence:

- Segment mix.
- Geographic mix.
- Disclosed major-customer relationships.
- Material contracts.
- Project and product dependencies.
- Relevant industry disclosures.

Do not equate segment diversification with proven customer diversification.

Do not interpret nondisclosure as proof of low concentration.

### 6.4 Pricing power

Review available evidence of:

- Ability to pass input costs to customers.
- Gross and operating margin stability.
- Demand response.
- Pricing behavior.
- Customer relationships.
- Non-price differentiation.

Margin stability alone does not establish pricing power.

Missing precise customer churn must not automatically block a defensible band.

### 6.5 Cyclicality and stress behavior

Review multi-year normalized operating performance.

Assess:

- Historical revenue changes.
- Earnings volatility.
- Relevant earnings drawdowns.
- Recovery behavior.
- Demand sensitivity.
- Operating leverage.
- Material cost pressures.
- Other relevant vulnerabilities.

Use retained evidence for relevant adverse periods.

Permit documented analyst scenarios where necessary and supported.

Do not fabricate an observed historical downturn.

Do not require a full internal corporate stress test.

For lower bands, evidence sufficiency must be proportionate to the actual band.

For maximum score, retain the existing requirement to demonstrate protection of franchise value and avoidance of distressed capital actions under severe plausible stress.

### 6.6 Preserve existing bands

Use exact existing canonical bands:

- 0: Structurally fragile.
- 1: Severe unmitigated dependence.
- 2: Material vulnerabilities.
- 3: Balanced resilience.
- 4: Strong diversified resilience.
- 5: Proven stress-resilient franchise.

Do not assign 3/5 as a default.

Do not require maximum-score stress evidence for a lower band.

Do not award maximum points without the approved maximum prerequisites and valid disconfirming evidence review.
