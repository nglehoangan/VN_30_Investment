## 7. FH-LIQ Method A

### 7.1 Economic objective

Assess the issuer's liquidity and refinancing risk profile using public financial evidence.

Do not require a direct certification of the ability to meet every financial obligation when due.

### 7.2 Core economic evidence

Assess:

- Cash buffer.
- Short-term interest-bearing debt.
- Interest-servicing capacity.
- Historical operating cash generation.
- Debt maturity concentration.
- Funding structure and refinancing history.

Supporting and conditional evidence:

- Book short-term deposits.
- Asset liquidity.
- Cash restriction disclosures.
- Guarantees.
- Covenants.
- Confirmed financial distress.
- Other applicable financial obligations.

Accept sector-equivalent evidence where appropriate.

Do not treat each ratio as an independently mandatory scoring operand.

Preserve existing economic anchors as analytical signals rather than automatic point conversion.

### 7.3 Method A bands

0 — CRITICAL_LIQUIDITY_DISTRESS

Evidence establishes critical liquidity distress, uncovered obligations or an inability to meet material financial commitments without credible mitigation.

Missing evidence alone is insufficient.

1 — WEAK_LIQUIDITY_RISK_PROFILE

Evidence establishes weak liquidity resources, material weakness in cash generation or servicing capacity, and severe dependence on external financing.

Do not assign solely because cash coverage is below a single threshold.

2 — MATERIAL_REFINANCING_RISK_WITH_FINANCIAL_CAPACITY

Evidence establishes meaningful financial capacity together with material refinancing or liquidity risk.

Financial capacity may be supported by observed financial resources, sustainable earnings, operating cash generation or demonstrated financing capacity.

Risk may be supported by low cash-only coverage, maturity concentration, funding dependence or deterioration in cash generation.

Do not require certification of full liquidity adequacy under Method B.

Unverified deposit accessibility may remain an explicit limitation if the risk-profile conclusion is independently supported.

3 — SOUND_LIQUIDITY_RISK_PROFILE

Evidence establishes a sound financial resource profile and manageable refinancing risk.

Require affirmative evidence of adequate financial buffers, cash generation and manageable funding dependence.

Material adverse contradictions must be resolved or reflected in a lower band.

4 — Not permitted for Method A.

Method A maximum: 3/4.

## 8. FH-STRESS Method A

### 8.1 Economic objective

Assess the severity of financial pressure and vulnerability under severe but plausible adverse economic scenarios.

Do not require exact verification of all future funding and capital actions to identify a supported vulnerability profile.

### 8.2 Evidence requirements

Reuse the existing stress model and retained financial data.

Assess:

- Revenue or sector-equivalent shock.
- Margin or earnings compression.
- Stressed operating profit.
- Stressed interest burden.
- Stressed interest coverage.
- Stressed operating cash generation.
- CAPEX and FCF implications.
- Cash buffer.
- Debt maturities.
- Funding sensitivity.
- Capital response implications.

Capital response detail is proportionate to the selected risk band.

Perform sensitivity analysis on material assumptions.

Do not count unsupported financing as available.

Do not present scenario outputs as observed corporate facts.

### 8.3 Method A bands

0 — CRITICAL_STRESS_FAILURE

Evidence establishes an inability to withstand a severe plausible stress scenario.

Require strong economic evidence.

Do not assign from missing information or an isolated extreme assumption.

1 — SEVERE_FINANCIAL_FRAGILITY

Evidence establishes very weak stressed financial capacity and serious dependence on potentially adverse financial actions.

Do not equate uncertain funding with confirmed distress.

2 — MATERIAL_STRESS_VULNERABILITY

Evidence establishes material financial pressure and vulnerability in a severe plausible stress scenario.

Relevant evidence includes:

- Significant deterioration in stressed cash generation.
- Material negative stressed FCF.
- Substantial debt maturity pressure.
- Material sensitivity to accessible financial buffers.
- Potential need to use reserves, adjust investment or obtain additional funding.

The analyst need not establish a specific future capital response or prove that the issuer will fail.

The vulnerability conclusion must be supported by actual stress calculations and economic evidence.

Uncertainty alone is insufficient.

3 — ADEQUATE_STRESS_BUFFER_WITH_MATERIAL_CONSTRAINTS

Evidence supports a meaningful financial buffer under stress, while material constraints remain.

Require affirmative evidence that the relevant financial pressures are economically manageable.

4 — STRONG_STRESS_RESILIENCE

Evidence supports strong stressed financial capacity, substantial buffers and manageable funding pressure.

Require meaningful robustness against plausible sensitivity ranges.

5 — Not permitted for Method A.

Method A maximum: 4/5.
