## 5. FH-BS — Balance-Sheet Strength

### 5.1 Economic objective

Assess the company's financial foundation, asset quality, leverage and ability to absorb losses.

Do not divide six points into independently additive metric scores.

Select one existing holistic assessment band.

### 5.2 Evidence Sufficiency contract

- Capital: CORE.
- Asset Quality: CORE.
- Leverage Context: CORE.
- Funding: CONDITIONAL or CORE according to sector and materiality.
- Strong normalized/downside capital buffers: MAXIMUM_ONLY for 6/6.

A missing immaterial supporting observation must not automatically block a defensible assessment.

Material funding dependencies and asset-quality uncertainties must remain explicitly assessed.

### 5.3 Required analytical calculations

For applicable non-financial issuers, calculate or assess:

- Cash and cash equivalents.
- Restricted versus unrestricted cash.
- Eligible financial deposits and liquid investments.
- Short-term interest-bearing debt.
- Long-term interest-bearing debt.
- Relevant lease obligations.
- Total interest-bearing debt.
- Net debt.
- Normalized EBIT.
- Normalized EBITDA.
- Net debt / normalized EBITDA.
- Debt / Equity where economically relevant.
- Asset composition.
- Receivables and recoverability.
- Material impairment and provisioning.
- Equity capital and loss-absorption capacity.
- Relevant guarantees and contingent financial obligations.

Use consistent definitions and consolidation scope.

Do not treat every listed metric as an independently mandatory scoring operand.

Select actual operands required by the defensible assessment route.

### 5.4 Existing economic anchors

Preserve existing Net Debt / EBITDA signals:

- Net cash: exceptional signal.
- 0–1x: strong.
- 1–2x: good.
- 2–3x: watch.
- 3–4x: weak.
- Above 4x: high risk.

These are economic signals, not automatic point assignments.

Do not infer 6/6 solely from a net-cash position.

Do not calculate misleading ratios using negative, near-zero or incompatible EBITDA.

Use alternative qualified economic evidence when applicable.

### 5.5 Approved public proxies

Permit:

- EBITDA reconstructed from qualified EBIT and depreciation/amortization.
- Receivable aging and provisioning as asset-quality evidence.
- Public collateral and secured-debt disclosures.
- Public funding structure and borrowing notes.
- Defensible recovery or capital-buffer ranges.

Do not invent asset recoverability, collateral values or unrestricted cash balances.

Exclude restricted cash from freely available resources.

Treat deposits and investments according to maturity, restrictions, currency, counterparty and accessibility.

### 5.6 Existing scoring bands

Preserve exact canonical bands:

| Points | Band |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Unacceptable |
| 1–2 | Weak |
| 3–4 | Adequate |
| 5–6 | Strong |

Preserve existing within-band stronger-evidence requirements.

Maximum 6/6 requires strong buffers under normalized and downside economics.

Preserve exceptional justification and disconfirming evidence review.

## 6. FH-LIQ — Liquidity and Refinancing

### 6.1 Economic objective

### 6.2 Evidence Sufficiency contract

- Obligations: CORE.
- Liquidity: CORE.
- Maturities: CORE.

- Funding diversification: BAND-DEPENDENT.

Preserve the existing approved FH-LIQ public proxy route.

Reuse its obligation/resource matching, covenant disposition and resource-overlap controls.

Do not create a second proxy specification.

### 6.3 Timed obligations

Identify material applicable obligations:

- Short-term borrowings.
- Current portion of long-term debt.
- Scheduled principal repayment.
- Interest payments.
- Material lease payments.
- Declared dividend obligations.
- Contractually committed CAPEX.
- Other material financial commitments.
- Guarantees or contingent obligations where relevant.

Use actual due dates or qualified maturity buckets.

Avoid double-counting an obligation already included in another category.

Do not assume all current liabilities are interest-bearing debt.

### 6.4 Usable liquidity

Identify:

- Unrestricted cash.
- Eligible short-term deposits.
- Qualified liquid financial assets.
- Committed undrawn facilities.
- Supported cash-generation scenarios.
- Other economically defensible funding resources.

Distinguish:

- Existing available resources.
- Contractually committed resources.
- Projected operating cash generation.
- Conditional refinancing.
- Uncommitted potential funding.

Do not count uncommitted credit lines as certain liquidity.

Do not count the same cash or deposit twice.

Do not count assets inaccessible to the relevant obligor as available liquidity.

Do not assume future positive CFO is guaranteed.

### 6.5 Coverage calculations

Preserve existing formula options:

Unrestricted cash / debt due within 12 months.

Or:

Qualified liquidity resources / obligations due within 12 months.

Use consistent numerator and denominator definitions.

Apply maturity timing, not only aggregate twelve-month totals.

Where exact intra-period timing is unavailable, use supported maturity buckets and conservative timing ranges.

Do not falsely certify precise day-by-day liquidity coverage.

Preserve existing anchors:

- At least 1.5x: strong.
- 1.0–1.5x: adequate.
- 0.75–1.0x: watch.
- Below 0.75x: weak unless refinancing visibility is exceptionally strong.

### 6.6 Interest coverage

Use qualified normalized EBIT / net interest expense where economically meaningful.

Preserve existing signals:

- At least 8x: strong.
- 5–8x: good.
- 3–5x: watch.
- 2–3x: weak.
- Below 2x: high risk.

If net interest expense is zero or negative:

- Do not generate an infinite ratio.
- Do not automatically assign maximum points.
- Review gross interest burden, financial resources and actual debt-servicing capacity.

Use approved sector-equivalent methods when necessary.

### 6.7 Covenant disposition

Use the exact existing approved proxy semantics where available.

Distinguish:

- Disclosed applicable covenant terms.
- Evidenced absence of relevant covenants.
- Evidenced non-applicability.
- Qualified nondisclosure.
- Potential or confirmed breach.

Exact covenant headroom is not mandatory for every assessment band.

Nondisclosure must not be treated as evidence that covenants are absent or satisfied.

Assess whether the unresolved covenant issue can materially change the liquidity conclusion.

Potential or confirmed breach must trigger the applicable materiality and risk review.

Preserve stricter maximum-score requirements.

Qualified nondisclosure alone cannot establish maximum-level covenant resilience.

### 6.8 Existing scoring bands

Preserve exact canonical bands:

| Points | Band |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Uncovered obligations without credible funding |
| 1 | Severe funding dependence |
| 2 | Adequate with material refinancing dependence |
| 3 | Sound coverage with manageable refinancing |
| 4 | Resilient liquidity and diversified funding |

Do not assign a low band because information is missing.

Do not award 4/4 from a single coverage ratio.

### 7.2 Evidence Sufficiency contract

- Funding Response: CORE.

### 7.3 Analyst scenario

For FPT, evaluate applicable drivers:

- Revenue contraction.
- Margin compression.
- Labor-cost pressure.
- Working-capital outflow.
- Interest-rate or funding-cost increase.
- Debt maturity requirements.
- Non-deferrable CAPEX.
- Accessible liquidity.
- Committed funding.
- Material guarantees and financial commitments.

Preserve existing non-financial stress examples, including revenue contraction of 15–25%, when economically appropriate.

Do not treat example shocks as universal fixed requirements.

Explain scenario severity and plausibility.

Use analyst estimates and conservative ranges where supported.

Clearly distinguish observed financial facts from analyst assumptions.

### 7.4 Required stress outputs

Calculate or assess:

- Base revenue.
- Stressed revenue.
- Base and stressed margins.
- Stressed EBIT.
- Stressed operating cash generation.
- Working-capital requirements.
- Interest burden.
- Mandatory capital expenditure.
- Debt principal payments.
- Available opening liquidity.
- Eligible funding resources.
- Resulting liquidity surplus or shortfall.
- Required capital response.

Use time-consistent cash-flow calculations.

Avoid double-counting interest, working capital, debt service or CAPEX.

Do not treat accounting profit as immediately available cash.

### 7.5 Funding and capital response

Determine whether the scenario implies:

- Survival using existing resources.
- Manageable expenditure adjustments.
- Ordinary committed financing.
- Material refinancing dependence.
- Emergency financing.
- Distressed asset sales.
- Emergency dilution.
- Failure to meet obligations.

Clearly qualify analyst assumptions about possible management responses.

Do not present unsupported management actions as guaranteed.

If scenario results are sensitive to material assumptions, provide bounded sensitivity analysis.

| Points | Band |

| 0 | Fails plausible stress |
| 1 | Likely distressed financing |
| 2 | Severe stress vulnerability |
| 3 | Survives with material constraints |
| 4 | Resilient with manageable constraints |
| 5 | Robust survival without distressed capital actions |

Maximum 5/5 requires robust survival under severe plausible stress without emergency dilution, distressed sales or damaging refinancing.
